Recognition

Honorable Mention at Fresh Forward.

A Spring 2023 group’s two-piece dress of bottle tabs and newspaper earned an Honorable Mention at Fresh Forward, the annual PVFA & SOA Student Exhibition, selected by guest judge Mark Chen.

The look was finished with a crown headpiece, bottle-tab earrings and a necklace. The group set out to show that fashion can be made from recyclable materials like newspaper, which hardly anyone reads anymore.

“Don’t let the world be old news with this fashionable newspaper dress. Recycle and reuse.”Group motto · Spring 2023

About the course.

In PERF 156: Dress in World Cultures, students study dress as more than fashion. Clothing becomes a way to understand how people across the world express identity, culture, belonging, memory, status and change.

Through discussion, research, reflection and hands-on making, students examine what dress means in global contexts while building critical thinking and intercultural communication skills.

The Waste Wearable Art project.

A collaborative project on fashion, sustainability and climate awareness. Groups source discarded materials, develop a concept, build the garment and present it at the end-of-semester runway show.

Instructor Dr. Grace Uchechukwu Adinku, Theatre & Performance Studies.

About the instructor

Dr. Grace Uchechukwu Adinku

Senior Lecturer, Theatre and Performance Studies
Texas A&M University

  • Costume design
  • African textiles
  • Cultural performance

My teaching and research explore how dress communicates identity, preserves cultural memory, and connects people to their communities and histories.

PERF 156: Dress in World Cultures

In this course, students explore dress and body practices across diverse societies, examining how culture, history, beliefs, social relationships, and the environment shape what people wear. I encourage students to look beyond appearance and understand dress as a meaningful expression of human experience.

The Waste Wearable Art project

One part of this learning is the Waste Wearable Art project, which challenges students to transform discarded materials into wearable designs while examining the environmental impact of fast fashion.

Through creative experimentation, students turn waste into a call to action, inviting us to rethink consumption, embrace repair and reuse, and make more thoughtful choices about what we wear and discard.
